Probability: 90% Toughness: Medium Problem-solving Study: 4 hrs

Appeared in every single paper examined. Both linear (graphical/tabular) and circular convolution asked repeatedly. Circular convolution is a staple 2-mark and 13-mark question across Reg 2019 and Reg 2023 papers.
Overlap-Add & Overlap-Save methods
Probability: 85% Toughness: Medium Problem-solving Study: 3 hrs
Frequency across papers85%
Appears as 12–13 mark questions in every assessment and end-semester. Both methods equally weighted. Overlap-save slightly more frequent in recent papers (Reg 2023).
LTI system properties — linearity, causality, stability, time-invariance
Probability: 80% Toughness: Easy Concept Study: 2 hrs
Frequency across papers80%
Consistent 2-mark and 6-mark question. "Check if system is linear, causal, stable" type numerical appears in every paper. Very high return on investment.
Energy & Power signals
Probability: 65% Toughness: Easy Formula Study: 1 hr
Frequency across papers65%
Frequent 2-mark question. "Check if x(n) = e^2n·u(n) is energy or power signal" is a near-verbatim repeat across multiple papers.

Most probable 13-mark questions

  • Use graphical method to compute the linear convolution of h[n]={2,0,−2,1,3} and x[n]={3,−2,0,1,2,5,−1,4}
  • Determine the output y(n) of an FIR filter with impulse response h(n)={1,1,1} for lengthy input x(n)={3,−1,0,1,3,2,0,1,2,1} using overlap-save method
  • Determine the convolution of x(n) and h(n) = δ(n)−δ(n−1)+δ(n−2)−δ(n−3) using graphical method. Also check system properties.

Most probable 8-mark questions

  • Check whether x(n) = u(n) − u(n−1) is energy or power signal
  • Determine if y(n) = 2x(n) + 1/x(n−1) is linear, time-invariant, causal and stable
  • Perform circular convolution of x(n)={1,2,2,1} and z(n)={2,3,1,1}

Unit II — Spectral Analysis of DT Signals and Systems
DFT computation — 8-point, N-point (direct + FFT)
Probability: 95% Toughness: Hard Problem-solving Study: 5 hrs
Frequency across papers95%
The single most repeated topic. 8-point DFT computation appears in every single paper without exception. Radix-2 DIT-FFT is the dominant algorithm tested. Parseval's theorem verification attached frequently.
Radix-2 DIT-FFT & DIF-FFT algorithms
Probability: 90% Toughness: Hard Problem-solving Study: 4 hrs
Frequency across papers90%
DIT and DIF both tested. DIT more common in assessments; DIF appeared in 2025 end-sem (IDFT using DIT-FFT). Also: count of multiplications/additions for N-point DFT vs FFT is a guaranteed 2-mark.
DTFT properties & computation
Probability: 80% Toughness: Medium Formula Study: 3 hrs
Frequency across papers80%
DTFT of 0.8^n·u(n) and using DTFT properties to derive related sequences (time shift, modulation, conjugation) is a recurring 12-mark pattern in recent Reg 2023 papers.
DFT properties — circular time shift, circular convolution
Probability: 75% Toughness: Medium Concept Study: 2 hrs
Frequency across papers75%
Circular time shift, twiddle factor properties, and DFT-IDFT method for linear convolution are consistent 2-mark and part questions.

Most probable 13-mark questions

  • Compute the 8-point DFT of x(n)={1,2,3,4,2,1} using radix-2 decimation-in-time FFT algorithm. Plot magnitude and phase spectrum.
  • Determine the 8-point IDFT using Radix-2 DIT-FFT algorithm given X(k)={20,−5.828−j2.279, 0,−0.172−j0.279, 0,−0.172+j0.279, 0,−5.828+j2.279}
  • Consider DTFT of {0.8^n u(n)} = 1/(1−0.8e^−jω). Compute DTFT of: i) 0.8^n u(n)cos(0.1πn), ii) n·0.8^n u(n), iii) 0.8^−n u(−n), iv) 0.8^n u(n−2)
  • Sample x(t)=1+cos2πt at 4 samples/sec. Determine 8-point DFT using DIT-FFT. Verify Parseval's theorem.

Most probable 8-mark questions

  • Calculate number of complex multiplications and additions for 64-point DFT using radix-2 FFT vs direct computation
  • State and prove circular time shift property of DFT
  • Find four-point DFT of x(n)={1,0,1,1} using radix-2 DIT-FFT algorithm
Low priority
Discrete Fourier series derivation Power spectral estimation (less frequent)
Unit III — Characterization of DT LTI Systems
Z-transform, ROC, and inverse Z-transform
Probability: 88% Toughness: Hard Problem-solving Study: 5 hrs
Frequency across papers88%
Heavy Z-transform numericals including partial fraction/long division inverse Z-transform, causal vs anticausal ROC, stability analysis. A definite 13-mark question. Appeared in every end-semester paper.
Transfer function from difference equations — stability and causality analysis
Probability: 85% Toughness: Medium Problem-solving Study: 3 hrs
Frequency across papers85%
Given difference equation → find H(z) → ROC → stability for causal/anticausal. Exact pattern repeats across Reg 2019 and Reg 2023 papers. Also: frequency response from difference equation is tested.
Z-transform properties — initial/final value, time reversal, shifting
Probability: 70% Toughness: Easy Memory Study: 1.5 hrs
Frequency across papers70%
2-mark questions on Z-transform properties are very consistent. Initial/final value theorem, time reversal, and the DTFT–Z-transform relationship are frequently tested.
Unit step/impulse response from difference equations
Probability: 75% Toughness: Medium Problem-solving Study: 3 hrs
Frequency across papers75%
Determine unit step response and impulse response using Z-transform with given initial conditions. Appeared verbatim in Reg 2023 Assessment Test-2 and end-sem.

Most probable 13-mark questions

  • A LTI system is characterized by H(z)=(3−4z⁻¹)/(1−3.5z⁻¹+1.5z⁻²). Specify ROC and find h(n) for: (i) causal, (ii) anticausal conditions. Comment on stability.
  • Obtain x(n) given X(z)={(1+1.16z⁻¹)/[(1−0.5z⁻¹)(1−0.33z⁻¹)]}; |z|>0.5
  • DT-LTI system: y(n)−y(n−1)+(3/16)y(n−2)=x(n)−(1/2)x(n−1). Comment on stability, sketch ROC for causal and anti-causal systems.
  • Determine the response of DT-LTI: y(n)=−0.8y(n−1)+x(n) for unit step input with initial conditions i) y(−1)=0, ii) y(−1)=2/9

Most probable 8-mark questions

  • Find Z-transform of x(n) = a^(n+1) u(n+1) and plot ROC
  • Find initial and final values of X(z) = (1+z⁻¹)/(1−0.25z⁻²)
  • Given H(z)=(z+0.3)/(z²−0.6z+0.08), determine system stability
Low priority
Relation between Z-transform and DTFT (theory only)
Unit IV — Design of IIR Filters
Bilinear Z-Transform (BZT) — Butterworth & Chebyshev digital filter design
Probability: 92% Toughness: Hard Problem-solving Study: 6 hrs
Frequency across papers92%
The highest-mark guaranteed topic across all papers. BZT is the dominant method for IIR filter design in ALL end-sems. Chebyshev Type-I via BZT appeared in 2025 end-sem as 13 marks. Always includes realization structure.
Butterworth analog filter design (order determination + poles + transfer function)
Probability: 88% Toughness: Hard Formula Study: 4 hrs
Frequency across papers88%
Design of Butterworth LPF from specs (passband gain, passband/stopband frequencies) is a staple 12-mark question in every assessment. The same spec values reappear — memorize the standard procedure.
Impulse Invariance method
Probability: 78% Toughness: Hard Formula Study: 4 hrs
Frequency across papers78%
Appeared as 13/14 mark question in Reg 2023 end-sem. Butterworth filter design using impulse invariance is a consistent alternative in Part-B. Also: "using IIM, find digital filter from H(s)=1/(s+1)²" is a 2-mark repeat.
IIR filter realization — Direct, Cascade, Parallel forms
Probability: 80% Toughness: Medium Concept Study: 2 hrs
Frequency across papers80%
Always part of the filter design question ("also obtain cascade/canonic realization"). Draw cascade form structure for given difference equation is a guaranteed sub-question in Part-C of end-sem.
Prewarping — why it is needed in BZT
Probability: 70% Toughness: Easy Memory Study: 0.5 hrs
Frequency across papers70%
2-mark definition question appearing consistently. "Why is prewarping needed?" or "What is the need for prewarping?" — direct repeat.

Most probable 13-mark questions

  • Using Bilinear Transformation, design a recursive digital Chebyshev Type-I filter with equiripple passband: 0.8≤|H(jω)|≤1 for 0≤ω≤0.2π; |H(jω)|≤0.2 for 0.6π≤ω≤π
  • Design a Butterworth digital LPF using BZT: Passband Edge 1kHz, Passband Ripple 2dB, Stopband Edge 3kHz, Stopband Ripple 20dB, Fs=8kHz. Obtain cascade and canonic realization.
  • Use Impulse Invariance to design Butterworth filter: 0.707≤|H(e^jω)|≤1 for 0≤ω≤0.5π; |H(e^jω)|≤0.2 for 0.75π≤ω≤π. T=1sec.
  • Design a Chebyshev Type-I analog LPF: αp=3dB, αs=16dB, fp=1kHz, fs=2kHz. Also obtain canonic and cascade realization.

Most probable 8-mark questions

  • Using IIM, obtain digital filter H(z) from H(s)=1/[(s+1)(s+2)]
  • Compute poles and find transfer function for 2nd-order prototype Butterworth filter
  • Draw cascade form structure for: y(n)=(3/4)y(n−1)−(1/8)y(n−2)+x(n)+(1/3)x(n−1)
Lower priority
Parallel form realization (standalone) Analog filter theory (concept only)
Unit V — Design of FIR Filters
FIR filter design using window functions (Hamming, Hanning, Rectangular)
Probability: 93% Toughness: Hard Problem-solving Study: 6 hrs
Frequency across papers93%
The single most tested FIR topic. Hamming window appears in every paper. 15-tap LPF/HPF/BPF design with window method is the dominant 13-mark question. Hanning window HPF also appears. Cutoff frequencies π/2, π/4, π/3, π/6 are the standard values.
Frequency sampling method — FIR filter coefficients from H(2πk/N)
Probability: 88% Toughness: Hard Problem-solving Study: 4 hrs
Frequency across papers88%
15-tap linear phase FIR with H(2πk/15) given as piecewise specification is a near-verbatim repeat across Reg 2019 and Reg 2023. Also asked in Part-C (compulsory question) in end-sems. Very high priority.
DFT-IDFT method for linear convolution using FIR filter
Probability: 80% Toughness: Medium Problem-solving Study: 3 hrs
Frequency across papers80%
Part-C compulsory question pattern. "FIR filter h(n)={1,2}; determine response for x(n)={1,2,7} using DFT-IDFT" appeared verbatim in multiple assessments.
Linear phase FIR structures — transversal and symmetric realization
Probability: 70% Toughness: Easy Concept Study: 1.5 hrs
Frequency across papers70%
Draw the linear phase realization for h(n)={1,2,3,4,3,2,1} or similar. Also: conditions for linear phase. High-value, low-effort 2-mark questions on Gibbs phenomenon and window selection.

Most probable 13-mark questions

  • Use Hamming window to design a 15-tap non-recursive linear phase LPF with cutoff frequency π/2 rad. Realize the transfer function.
  • Use Hanning window to design a 15-tap non-recursive linear phase HPF with cutoff frequency π/6 rad. Realize the transfer function of the designed filter.
  • Determine coefficients of a linear phase FIR filter of length M=15 with symmetric unit sample response satisfying H(2πk/15): 1 for k=0,1,2,3; 0.6 for k=4; 0 for k=5,6,7
  • Design and realize a non-recursive FIR filter using Hamming window for H(e^jω) = e^−j5ω for |ω|≤π/3 and 0 otherwise

Most probable 8-mark questions

  • Determine the length of FIR filter and window required for stopband attenuation between 24dB and 40dB, transition band 200Hz, sampling frequency 2kHz
  • What is Gibbs phenomenon? How to avoid it in FIR filter design?
  • Draw the linear phase realization for h(n)={1,2,3,4,3,2,1}
Lower priority
Comparison of FIR vs IIR (theory only) Kaiser window (rarely tested)
